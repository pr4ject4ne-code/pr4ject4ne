# Walls of importance (Walls of importance_260311_123910.pdf, 8 pages + 1 blank)

## Purpose
Saved AI-advice conversation on go-to-market strategy. Opens: "Yes. Validating demand **before building the product** is the correct approach." It is the strategic playbook the founder is following (the year-1 goals in the $1B-pattern doc mirror it). Dated earliest of the six (2603xx), so it predates and likely shaped the pillars.

## Why pre-validation (as given)
1. "Hospitals are slow adopters" — risk-averse institutions need clear operational value.
2. "The real problem may differ from assumptions" — clinics' biggest problems may be: "appointment chaos, patient no-shows, long waiting times, poor staff coordination." "Validation ensures you build what they **will actually pay for**."
3. "Early buyers guide product design" — feature priorities, workflow design, integration needs.

## Recommended validation strategy
1. Interview decision-makers: hospital administrators, clinic managers, senior physicians running private clinics. Ask about **operational pain points, not your solution** (e.g., "How are appointments currently managed?", "What causes the biggest delays in patient flow?", "What software do you currently use?").
2. Test willingness to pay: "If a platform reduced waiting time and automated appointments, would your clinic pay a monthly fee?"
3. Validate specific features individually: "online appointment booking, digital patient intake forms, doctor scheduling dashboard, queue monitoring, operational analytics" — find which creates the **strongest interest**.
4. Secure pilot partners: ideal outcome is "**2–5 clinics willing to pilot the system** once it exists," framed as early partner program / discounted subscription / feature customization → "initial customers before development."

## Recommended MVP (concrete)
> "Clinic workflow and appointment system. Core features: appointment booking, digital patient intake, doctor schedule dashboard, queue tracking. **Avoid building the AI layer initially. The AI analytics can be added later after enough operational data exists.**"

## Pricing guidance
- SaaS: small clinic = monthly subscription; mid-size private hospital = higher subscription based on number of doctors. "The exact price should come from **interviews with clinics**."

## Strategic rules
- Focus on **one geographic region first** (easier meetings, faster onboarding, direct support).
- Build relationships before selling — healthcare runs on "trust and reputation."
- Solve a clear operational pain: save time, reduce admin work, increase patient throughput.
- Strong opportunity: "Many private clinics still use **paper appointment books or basic messaging tools**."
- "The Most Important Rule: Do not build features unless at least three clinics explicitly request them."
- Key question to answer via interviews: "**What is the single most frustrating daily operational problem for clinic managers?**"
- "Your biggest advantage will not be technology. It will be deep understanding of clinic workflow in your local environment."
- Teaser at end: a specific clinic segment "almost always adopts systems like this faster" — segment not named in this doc.
