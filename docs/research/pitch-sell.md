# Pitch-SELL_260331_231337.pdf (10 pages)

## Purpose
Two versions of a ~2-minute verbal sales pitch: (A) a field pitch for clinics, (B) a tighter "decision-maker-level" pitch for hospital owners/admins. Follow-up to Pitch-PRICING's named next step.

## What the product is (as pitched)
"A hospital workflow system that organizes the entire outpatient process—from appointment booking to consultation." Patients book appointments, see real-time queue positions, arrive at the right time; doctors get a clear view of their patient lineup before consultations; admin staff manage scheduling, tasks, and overall clinic flow from a single dashboard.

## Problem claimed
"Most private clinics still manage appointments, patient flow, and staff coordination manually or across disconnected tools. This leads to long waiting times, overcrowding, and doctors not having clear visibility of their daily lineup."

## Value promised (three immediate improvements)
1. Reduces patient waiting time and overcrowding
2. Helps doctors prepare and move faster through consultations
3. Gives management visibility over daily operations
"So instead of reacting to chaos, the clinic runs in a structured, predictable way."

## Differentiation vs competitors (only competitive claim in these docs)
"Most existing systems focus mainly on record-keeping. This is focused on workflow and patient flow, which is where most clinics actually struggle day-to-day."

## Pitch A structure (clinic version)
1. Opening / problem recognition (20–30s) 2. Solution (30–40s) 3. Key benefits tied to outcomes (30–40s) 4. Soft pricing anchor, optional (10–15s): "We're structuring pricing to scale with clinic size, so smaller clinics aren't overcharged" — keep it light. 5. Close with low-risk next step (20–30s): pilot invitation as "early partner".

## Pitch B structure (decision-maker version)
1) Problem 15–20s, 2) Solution 25–30s, 3) Key value 30–40s, 4) Differentiation 20–25s, 5) Low-risk entry 20–25s ("try it in your workflow for a short period at no cost, then continue on a discounted plan if it adds value"), 6) Close 10–15s: "Would you be open to testing something like this in your clinic if it improves how your daily operations run?"

## 30-second compressed version (verbatim)
"We're building a system that helps clinics manage appointments, patient flow, and doctor scheduling in one place. It reduces waiting time, helps doctors see their lineup ahead, and makes daily operations more organized. We're currently onboarding a few clinics for pilot testing would you be open to trying it if it fits your workflow?"

## Delivery rules
Keep conversational; stop and listen; adapt in real time (waiting time → emphasize queue; admin stress → emphasize dashboards; patient complaints → emphasize experience). What NOT to do: list too many features, **mention AI early**, sound too technical, rush into pricing, talk too long without feedback.

## Goal of the pitch (decision)
Not to close a sale — win = "This sounds useful. Let's talk more."
