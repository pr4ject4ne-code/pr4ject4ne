## Purpose
NOT a record of interviews conducted — it is an interview question guide/script (reads as AI-generated advice) for customer-discovery interviews with clinics. No interviewees, no answers recorded.

## Structure
Extract three types of information: current workflow and pain points; willingness to adopt software; conditions to become early customers. Questions move understanding -> validation -> commitment.

1. Understanding current clinic operations — booking method, walk-in vs scheduled, busiest time, delay causes; paper vs digital records, which system, ease of retrieving prior records; reception staffing, registration time, most repetitive admin tasks; whether doctors see full patient list before clinic hours, how often schedules run behind, biggest schedule disruptor.
2. Identifying pain points — most frustrating part of running the clinic, most time-consuming admin task, most frequent patient complaints, patients leaving over wait times, lost patient files, missed appointments.
3. Testing the core idea — pitch script: "I'm exploring a system that allows patients to book appointments online, lets doctors see their daily patient lineup ahead of time, and helps clinics track patient flow and waiting times." Then: would it help, which part is most useful, must-have features, concerns.
4. Testing willingness to pay — would you pay, expected monthly cost, subscription vs one-time payment.
5. Technology readiness — computers at reception, mobile app vs computer system preference, reliable internet, openness to storing patient data digitally.
6. Pilot partnership — interest in testing, early-partner willingness, long-term use, referrals to other clinics.
7. Market expansion — do nearby clinics share the problems, other interested clinic managers, "are there specific regulations or policies I should be aware of when building such software?"

## Best practices stated
Speak with clinic managers, not just receptionists; listen first, don't pitch immediately; take notes on recurring problems. "If 5-10 clinics mention the same problem, that problem should become the core feature of your product." Always end with: "If this system solved your biggest administrative problem, would you be willing to try it when it's ready?" — several yeses = first customers. Ends with an offer (from the AI) to list "the 10 features your first version must include (and the 20 you should avoid building initially)."
